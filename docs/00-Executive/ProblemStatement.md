# Problem Statement

> **Purpose**
>
> This document defines the business problems that the Project Management Platform is intended to solve. It establishes the need for the product, identifies challenges faced by production-driven organizations, and explains why existing solutions are insufficient.

---

# Executive Summary

Creative production organizations often rely on a collection of disconnected tools to manage projects, production, communication, reviews, resource planning, and financial operations.

While each tool performs its individual function effectively, the lack of integration creates operational inefficiencies, limited visibility, duplicated work, inconsistent data, and delayed decision-making.

The Project Management Platform aims to provide a unified, workflow-driven solution that centralizes production management while remaining highly configurable to support different business processes.

---

# Current Industry Challenges

Most organizations use multiple independent systems such as:

- CRM Software
- Project Management Tools
- Production Tracking Systems
- Spreadsheet-based Planning
- Communication Platforms
- Source Control Systems
- File Sharing Platforms
- Accounting Software
- Business Intelligence Tools

These systems often operate independently, requiring manual coordination and synchronization.

---

# Key Business Problems

## 1. Fragmented Production Management

Production activities are spread across multiple systems.

Example:

- Client information exists in one application.
- Project tracking exists in another.
- Production status is maintained in spreadsheets.
- Reviews are managed through email or chat.
- Financial information is maintained separately.

This fragmentation results in inconsistent data and poor operational visibility.

---

## 2. Lack of End-to-End Visibility

Management cannot easily answer questions such as:

- What is the current production status?
- Which assets are delayed?
- Which batches are behind schedule?
- What is awaiting client review?
- What work is currently under review?
- Which projects are financially at risk?

Obtaining these answers often requires collecting information from multiple teams and systems.

---

## 3. Manual Workflow Management

Many production workflows rely on manual coordination.

Examples include:

- Sending work for review.
- Assigning reviewers.
- Tracking approvals.
- Managing feedback.
- Informing stakeholders.
- Updating status manually.

This increases the risk of delays, missed approvals, and inconsistent execution.

---

## 4. Inefficient Review Process

Review cycles are often managed through emails, chat applications, or informal communication.

Common issues include:

- Feedback is scattered across multiple channels.
- Review history is difficult to track.
- Previous review rounds are not preserved.
- Responsibility for implementing feedback is unclear.
- Review progress lacks transparency.

This results in unnecessary rework and extended delivery timelines.

---

## 5. Poor Resource Planning

Resource allocation is frequently managed using spreadsheets or manual planning.

Organizations struggle to answer questions such as:

- Who is available?
- Who is overallocated?
- Who has remaining capacity?
- Which batch requires additional resources?

Without centralized planning, resource utilization becomes inefficient.

---

## 6. Limited Production Tracking

Traditional project management systems focus primarily on tasks.

Production environments require additional concepts such as:

- Assets
- Deliverables
- Production Stages
- Review Rounds
- Workflow Processes
- Batch Planning

Most generic tools do not adequately represent these production concepts.

---

## 7. Inconsistent Workflow Execution

Different teams often follow different procedures for similar work.

Examples include:

- Different review sequences
- Different approval rules
- Different production stages
- Different status definitions

Without standardized workflows, consistency and governance become difficult.

---

## 8. Lack of Traceability

Organizations frequently struggle to determine:

- Who changed a task?
- When was work approved?
- Who assigned the reviewer?
- What feedback resulted in rework?
- Which version was delivered?
- What files were submitted?

Missing audit information creates operational and compliance challenges.

---

## 9. Communication Scattered Across Platforms

Discussions occur across multiple channels including:

- Email
- Chat applications
- Meetings
- Personal messages

Important production information becomes disconnected from the actual work being performed.

---

## 10. Weak Integration Between Production and Finance

Production and financial information are rarely connected.

Examples include:

- Project progress is not linked to invoicing.
- Outstanding invoices are not visible from project dashboards.
- Billing milestones are manually tracked.
- Financial reporting requires manual reconciliation.

---

## 11. Limited Support for Creative Production

Creative production introduces challenges not addressed by generic task management systems.

Examples include:

- Asset lifecycle management
- Multiple review rounds
- Client review cycles
- Final review approvals
- QC validation
- Deliverable tracking
- Source control integration

These concepts require dedicated production workflows.

---

## 12. Insufficient Operational Intelligence

Most organizations rely on historical reports rather than real-time operational insights.

Management requires answers to questions such as:

- Which projects are at risk?
- Which teams are overloaded?
- Which review stage causes delays?
- What is the average review turnaround time?
- Which workflow produces the most rework?

Existing systems provide limited analytical capability.

---

# Why Existing Solutions Are Not Enough

Current market solutions generally focus on a single business area.

Examples include:

| Category | Limitation |
|----------|------------|
| Generic Project Management | Limited production-specific workflows |
| Production Tracking Systems | Limited business management capabilities |
| Spreadsheet Planning | Difficult to maintain and scale |
| Communication Platforms | No structured production context |
| Finance Systems | No production awareness |
| Source Control | No project or workflow management |

Organizations often compensate by integrating multiple products, increasing complexity and maintenance effort.

# Problem Statement Summary

Production-driven organizations require more than a traditional project management tool.

They need a unified platform that understands production workflows, supports configurable business processes, centralizes operational data, and provides complete visibility across the entire project lifecycle.

The Project Management Platform is designed to address these challenges while providing a scalable foundation for future automation and AI-driven capabilities.
